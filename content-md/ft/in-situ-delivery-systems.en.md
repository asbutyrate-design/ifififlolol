# Pharmaceutical development of stimulus-sensitive in situ delivery systems: composition, technology, standardisation and biopharmaceutical evaluation

Project team: PhD (Doctor of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Professor E.O. Bakhrushina; Senior Lecturer I.B. Mikhel; Assistant V.S. Pyzhov; Assistant M.V. Pomytkina; postgraduate students: N.O. Belyavsky, P.S. Sakharova, A.K. Kartashova; residents: E.V. Kashevarova; students: Ya.A. Avdonina, G.A. Gribanov, K.S. Leonova, E.S. Panova, A.A. Kasatikova, S.B. Konon, A.N. Golub.

The project is carried out jointly with D.I. Mendeleev Russian University of Chemical Technology, the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, and the Gamaleya Research Centre for Epidemiology and Microbiology.

## Project description

Project goal: comprehensive development of stimulus-sensitive drug delivery systems, including the selection and substantiation of active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients, formulation design, selection of the optimal technology, standardisation across all quality attributes, and scaling up of the manufacturing process to ensure reproducibility and stability of the finished product.

## Key objectives

- Determining the relevance of developing stimulus-sensitive drug delivery systems for various fields of application, and weighing the advantages and disadvantages of this dosage form.
- Analysing active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients for incorporation into stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including assessment of their compatibility with polymer matrices.
- Developing formulations and technological schemes for producing stimulus-sensitive delivery systems for various fields of application (ophthalmology, dentistry, intranasal administration, implantation, etc.).
- Selecting and comparing polymer matrices, including biodegradable ones, for creating thermosensitive, ion-triggered, pH-sensitive, photosensitive and phase-inversion systems.
- Developing and studying domestically produced stimulus-sensitive polymers, including evaluation of their physicochemical, rheological and biopharmaceutical properties.
- Developing and validating original biorelevant methods for assessing the quality of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including methods for monitoring release, gelation and stability.
- Developing in vitro models to determine the biopharmaceutical parameters of in situ systems, including models simulating the conditions of target biological media.
- Establishing in vitro–in vivo correlation (IVIVC) for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems and confirming the predictive value of the developed in vitro models.
- Optimising formulations by various methods of mathematical design (QbD, DOE) to achieve the specified quality attributes.
- Scientifically substantiating quality attributes and their test methods for inclusion in regulatory documentation for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including the development of approaches to specification setting and acceptance criteria.
- Developing a risk-based methodology for assessing critical process parameters and quality attributes of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems based on risk analysis.

## Expected results

- A substantiated selection of active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients for incorporation into stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including data on compatibility with polymer matrices.
- Developed and optimised formulations of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems for various fields of application: thermosensitive, ion-triggered, pH-sensitive, photosensitive and phase-inversion systems.
- Domestically produced stimulus-sensitive polymers with characterised physicochemical, rheological and biopharmaceutical properties, suitable for creating delivery systems.
- Original biorelevant methods for assessing the quality of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, validated and suitable for standardisation.
- In vitro models for determining the biopharmaceutical parameters of in situ systems, simulating the conditions of target biological media and enabling prediction of the system's behaviour in the body.
- Established in vitro–in vivo correlations (IVIVC) for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, confirming the predictive value of the developed in vitro models.
- A technological production scheme and equipment design for the process of obtaining stimulus-sensitive delivery systems.
- Scientifically substantiated quality attributes and their test methods, proposed for inclusion in regulatory documentation, with justified acceptance criteria.
- A risk-based methodology for assessing critical process parameters and quality attributes of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including risk maps and justification of critical control points.
- Scientifically substantiated recommendations on the use of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems in various fields of medicine and pharmacy.
