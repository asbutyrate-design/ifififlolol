# Development, standardisation and scaling of the manufacturing technology of medicinal products in the form of chewing gum with local or systemic action

Project team: PhD (Doctor of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Professor E.O. Bakhrushina; PhD (Candidate of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Associate Professor S.I. Kosenkova; student: Yu.A. Saksonova.

The project is carried out jointly with D.I. Mendeleev Russian University of Chemical Technology and the Federal Research Centre for Original and Promising Biomedical and Pharmaceutical Technologies.

## Project description

Project goal: Comprehensive development of medicinal products in the form of medicated chewing gum, including the selection and substantiation of active substances and excipients, formulation design, selection of the optimal technology and standardisation across all quality attributes. Scaling up of the manufacturing process to ensure reproducibility and stability of the finished product.

## Key objectives

- determining the relevance of developing medicated chewing gum and weighing the advantages and disadvantages of this dosage form;
- analysing active substances for incorporation into chewing gum;
- developing formulations and technological production schemes for chewing gums;
- selecting the chewing base, including the use and comparison of biodegradable bases;
- developing methods for correcting the unpleasant taste of active substances and excipients;
- optimising formulations by various methods of mathematical design;
- selecting and creating optimal technological equipment for conducting the "Dissolution/chewing" test.

Scientific project: "New generation transmucosal delivery: creation of applicative mucoadhesive systems"
Project team: PhD, Professor Anurova M.N., PhD, Associate Professor Korol V.A., PhD student Petrulenko K.E, graduate student Kulmin A.R., resident Ivanova A.N.

Project description: The aim of the project is to develop an innovative platform of applicative mucoadhesive dosage forms for effective local and systemic drug delivery, providing increased bioavailability, prolonged release, and protection of unstable drugs.

## Key tasks

- to develop scientific approaches to the design of applicative dosage forms with specified mucoadhesive and kinetic characteristics;
- to investigate the mechanisms of interaction of mucoadhesive systems with mucous membranes;
- to conduct comprehensive in vitro and in vivo studies of release kinetics, permeability, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and therapeutic efficacy;
- to create a scientific and methodological base for the development of a platform of mucoadhesive application forms, including models for predicting properties and optimizing composition;
- to create a line of import-independent applicative mucoadhesive dosage forms (sprays, films, gels) for the systemic and local delivery of synthetic and biotechnological substances with subsequent technology transfer to industrial production and clinical practice.

Development of Injectable Dosage Forms of Antitumour Drugs Taking into Account Modern Methods of Active Substance Delivery

Project team: PhD, Professor I. I. Krasnyuk; PhD, Associate Professor Zh. M. Kozlova; postgraduate student S. D. Shcheglov; student R. A. Bandin.

## Project description

Project goal: Pharmaceutical development of injectable dosage forms of antitumor drugs based on adaptive liposomal delivery systems (including biomimetic and targeted ones) that provide an increase in the half-life of the active substance in the body and targeted delivery to the tumor node to improve the efficacy of chemotherapy of malignant neoplasms.

## Key objectives of the project

 Development of scientific approaches to the design of liposomal nanoplatforms for the delivery of antitumor drugs (including functionalization with biomimetic and targeted ligands)
 Study of the mechanisms of encapsulation of antitumor substances in liposomes and the influence of composition factors on their efficiency of incorporation into vesicles and validation of the structure of the nanosized delivery system
 Optimization of technological approaches to the design of liposomal dosage forms with subsequent scaling of the technology
 Conducting comprehensive in vitro and in vivo studies of the stability of liposomal dosage forms, release kinetics, as well as their cytotoxic potential, biodistribution, and metabolic excretion pathways
 Creation of a scientific and methodological basis for the development of lipid-based nanoplatforms, including models for predicting properties, composition, and required functionalization for a clinical task
