Name of the scientific project: "Pharmaceutical development of stimulus-sensitive in situ delivery systems: composition, technology, standardisation and biopharmaceutical evaluation"

Project team: PhD (Doctor of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Professor E.O. Bakhrushina; Senior Lecturer I.B. Mikhel; Assistant V.S. Pyzhov; Assistant M.V. Pomytkina; postgraduate students: N.O. Belyavsky, P.S. Sakharova, A.K. Kartashova; residents: E.V. Kashevarova; students: Ya.A. Avdonina, G.A. Gribanov, K.S. Leonova, E.S. Panova, A.A. Kasatikova, S.B. Konon, A.N. Golub.

The project is carried out jointly with D.I. Mendeleev Russian University of Chemical Technology, the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, and the Gamaleya Research Centre for Epidemiology and Microbiology.

Project description:
Project goal: comprehensive development of stimulus-sensitive drug delivery systems, including the selection and substantiation of active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients, formulation design, selection of the optimal technology, standardisation across all quality attributes, and scaling up of the manufacturing process to ensure reproducibility and stability of the finished product.

Key objectives:
- Determining the relevance of developing stimulus-sensitive drug delivery systems for various fields of application, and weighing the advantages and disadvantages of this dosage form.
- Analysing active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients for incorporation into stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including assessment of their compatibility with polymer matrices.
- Developing formulations and technological schemes for producing stimulus-sensitive delivery systems for various fields of application (ophthalmology, dentistry, intranasal administration, implantation, etc.).
- Selecting and comparing polymer matrices, including biodegradable ones, for creating thermosensitive, ion-triggered, pH-sensitive, photosensitive and phase-inversion systems.
- Developing and studying domestically produced stimulus-sensitive polymers, including evaluation of their physicochemical, rheological and biopharmaceutical properties.
- Developing and validating original biorelevant methods for assessing the quality of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including methods for monitoring release, gelation and stability.
- Developing in vitro models to determine the biopharmaceutical parameters of in situ systems, including models simulating the conditions of target biological media.
- Establishing in vitro–in vivo correlation (IVIVC) for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems and confirming the predictive value of the developed in vitro models.
- Optimising formulations by various methods of mathematical design (QbD, DOE) to achieve the specified quality attributes.
- Scientifically substantiating quality attributes and their test methods for inclusion in regulatory documentation for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including the development of approaches to specification setting and acceptance criteria.
- Developing a risk-based methodology for assessing critical process parameters and quality attributes of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems based on risk analysis.

Expected results:
- A substantiated selection of active pharmaceutical ingredients and excipients for incorporation into stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including data on compatibility with polymer matrices.
- Developed and optimised formulations of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems for various fields of application: thermosensitive, ion-triggered, pH-sensitive, photosensitive and phase-inversion systems.
- Domestically produced stimulus-sensitive polymers with characterised physicochemical, rheological and biopharmaceutical properties, suitable for creating delivery systems.
- Original biorelevant methods for assessing the quality of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, validated and suitable for standardisation.
- In vitro models for determining the biopharmaceutical parameters of in situ systems, simulating the conditions of target biological media and enabling prediction of the system's behaviour in the body.
- Established in vitro–in vivo correlations (IVIVC) for stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, confirming the predictive value of the developed in vitro models.
- A technological production scheme and equipment design for the process of obtaining stimulus-sensitive delivery systems.
- Scientifically substantiated quality attributes and their test methods, proposed for inclusion in regulatory documentation, with justified acceptance criteria.
- A risk-based methodology for assessing critical process parameters and quality attributes of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems, including risk maps and justification of critical control points.
- Scientifically substantiated recommendations on the use of stimulus-sensitive delivery systems in various fields of medicine and pharmacy.

Name of the scientific project: "Development, standardisation and scaling of the manufacturing technology of medicinal products in the form of chewing gum with local or systemic action"

Project team: PhD (Doctor of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Professor E.O. Bakhrushina; PhD (Candidate of Pharmaceutical Sciences), Associate Professor S.I. Kosenkova; student: Yu.A. Saksonova.

The project is carried out jointly with D.I. Mendeleev Russian University of Chemical Technology and the Federal Research Centre for Original and Promising Biomedical and Pharmaceutical Technologies.

Project description:
Project goal: Comprehensive development of medicinal products in the form of medicated chewing gum, including the selection and substantiation of active substances and excipients, formulation design, selection of the optimal technology and standardisation across all quality attributes. Scaling up of the manufacturing process to ensure reproducibility and stability of the finished product.

Key objectives:
- determining the relevance of developing medicated chewing gum and weighing the advantages and disadvantages of this dosage form;
- analysing active substances for incorporation into chewing gum;
- developing formulations and technological production schemes for chewing gums;
- selecting the chewing base, including the use and comparison of biodegradable bases;
- developing methods for correcting the unpleasant taste of active substances and excipients;
- optimising formulations by various methods of mathematical design;
- selecting and creating optimal technological equipment for conducting the "Dissolution/chewing" test.

Scientific project: "New generation transmucosal delivery: creation of applicative mucoadhesive systems"
Project team: PhD, Professor Anurova M.N., PhD, Associate Professor Korol V.A., PhD student Petrulenko K.E, graduate student Kulmin A.R., resident Ivanova A.N.


Project description: The aim of the project is to develop an innovative platform of applicative mucoadhesive dosage forms for effective local and systemic drug delivery, providing increased bioavailability, prolonged release, and protection of unstable drugs.
Key tasks:
- to develop scientific approaches to the design of applicative dosage forms with specified mucoadhesive and kinetic characteristics; 
- to investigate the mechanisms of interaction of mucoadhesive systems with mucous membranes;
- to conduct comprehensive in vitro and in vivo studies of release kinetics, permeability, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and therapeutic efficacy; 
- to create a scientific and methodological base for the development of a platform of mucoadhesive application forms, including models for predicting properties and optimizing composition;
- to create a line of import-independent applicative mucoadhesive dosage forms (sprays, films, gels) for the systemic and local delivery of synthetic and biotechnological substances with subsequent technology transfer to industrial production and clinical practice.

Development of Injectable Dosage Forms of Antitumour Drugs Taking into Account Modern Methods of Active Substance Delivery

Project team: PhD, Professor I. I. Krasnyuk; PhD, Associate Professor Zh. M. Kozlova; postgraduate student S. D. Shcheglov; student R. A. Bandin.
Project description: 
Project goal: Pharmaceutical development of injectable dosage forms of antitumor drugs based on adaptive liposomal delivery systems (including biomimetic and targeted ones) that provide an increase in the half-life of the active substance in the body and targeted delivery to the tumor node to improve the efficacy of chemotherapy of malignant neoplasms. 
Key objectives of the project:
	Development of scientific approaches to the design of liposomal nanoplatforms for the delivery of antitumor drugs (including functionalization with biomimetic and targeted ligands)
	Study of the mechanisms of encapsulation of antitumor substances in liposomes and the influence of composition factors on their efficiency of incorporation into vesicles and validation of the structure of the nanosized delivery system
	Optimization of technological approaches to the design of liposomal dosage forms with subsequent scaling of the technology
	Conducting comprehensive in vitro and in vivo studies of the stability of liposomal dosage forms, release kinetics, as well as their cytotoxic potential, biodistribution, and metabolic excretion pathways
	Creation of a scientific and methodological basis for the development of lipid-based nanoplatforms, including models for predicting properties, composition, and required functionalization for a clinical task
