"Taxonomy and resources of plants promising for use in medicine"
 
Project team: Head of the Department of Pharmaceutical Natural Sciences, Dr. Pharm. Sci. Luferov A.N., postgraduate student Melnik A.A., student Tinkt A.V. 
Project description: 
Project objective: Study and analysis of the taxonomic composition, distribution and productivity of plants, increasing the efficiency of using their resources for practical pharmacy. 
Key tasks: 
- Study of seed plant biodiversity to identify taxonomically significant features; 
- Determination of the species composition and systematic position of seed plants; compilation of keys for their identification; 
- Study of geographical features of species; compilation of point maps of their ranges; 
- Conducting an assessment of promising areas of use of seed plants in medical practice.

NAME OF THE SCIENTIFIC PROJECT: “New types of medicinal plant raw materials: from handling to regulatory documentation”
Project leader: Doctor of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Professor Sergunova E.V.
Project team: Ph.D. Associate Professor Kovaleva T.Yu., Ph.D. senior teacher Dorovskikh E.A., graduate student Pavlenko A.A., graduate student Tarasova A.V., graduate student Melnik A.A.
Project Goal
To identify and scientifically substantiate promising types of medicinal plant raw materials for further use in pharmaceutical practice — including standardization, development of regulatory documentation, and creation of new medicinal plant based drugs.

Project Tasks
1.	Conduct a screening of botanical taxa based on ethnobotanical data, traditional medical systems, and modern publications to select species with potentially high biological activity and a sufficient geographical distribution.
2.	Collect and document raw material samples in accordance with herbarium and traceability protocols: record the collection site and time, phenophase, and environmental conditions, while ensuring the retention of duplicate samples for analytical and control studies.
3.	Perform botanical identification using morphological, microscopic, and molecular-genetic methods to rule out species misidentification and potential instances of adulteration or substitution.
4.	Analyze the chemical composition of the selected samples using chromatography, spectroscopy, and other appropriate analytical approaches to isolate key groups of biologically active substances (BAS) and quantify their content.
5.	Evaluate pharmacological activity (in vitro and, where facilities permit, in vivo) using relevant models—such as antioxidant, antimicrobial, nootropic, or anti-inflammatory activity—depending on the raw material's profile.
6.	Develop and validate quality control methods for specific types of medicinal plant raw materials, establishing parameters for authenticity, quality standards, and the quantification of active constituents.
7.	Compile a database of the studied species, integrating botanical, chemical, pharmacological, and technological information, including recommendations for harvesting, drying, storage, and potential applications.
8.	Prepare draft regulatory documents (e.g., draft pharmacopoeial monographs) and methodological guidelines to facilitate further research and the practical implementation of the findings.

Project Description
The Department of Pharmaceutical Natural Sciences is implementing this project as an interdisciplinary initiative at the intersection of pharmacognosy, botany, pharmaceutical chemistry, and pharmacology. It is grounded in a systems approach to identifying and studying new sources of medicinal plant raw materials, spanning the entire process from the field stage (collection and identification) to laboratory analysis (chemical analysis and activity testing) and regulatory work (development of quality control methods).
A distinctive feature of the project is its focus on the practical applicability of the results: each species is evaluated not only for biological activity but also in terms of resource availability (distribution and cultivation potential), processing feasibility, and safety. This approach enables the selection of plants that are not merely "active" but hold genuine promise for the pharmaceutical industry.
The project findings will be used for scientific publications and the department's educational materials, as well as to support research grant applications and collaborations with industry partners in the development of herbal medicines and dietary supplements.

Research Project Title: “Creation of a Database of Ionomic Profiles of Medicinal Plant Raw Materials and Preparations Based on Them”

Project Team: Dr. of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Professor I.V. Gravel; postgraduate students: D.V. Levushkin, V.N. Shestopalova, O.S. Lavrova.
The project is carried out jointly with the Center for Bioelementology and Human Ecology of the Federal State Autonomous Educational Institution of Higher Education “I.M. Sechenov First Moscow State Medical University” of the Ministry of Health of Russia (Sechenov University) and LLC “Analytical and Research Instruments”.
Project Description:
Project Aim: Study of the elemental profiles of herbal medicinal preparations and raw materials as potential sources of macro- and microelements for humans.
Key Objectives:
•	Study of the composition of essential, conditionally essential, and toxic elements in medicinal plant raw materials and herbal medicinal preparations  of various types.
•	Development and validation of alternative methods for pharmacopoeial elemental analysis, including sample preparation and instrumental determination.
•	Assessment of the bioavailability of elements from HMPs by in vitro and in vivo methods.
•	Comparative safety assessment of the use of MPRM and HMPs based on the content of toxic elements.
•	Development of recommendations for the use of the elemental complex in HMPs in the prevention and treatment of dyselementoses.

Scientific Project Title: "Glycans of Medicinal Plant Raw Materials"
Ph.D. in Pharmaceutical Sciences, Associate Professor D.O. Bokov
The project is carried out at the Department of Pharmaceutical Natural Sciences, Institute of Pharmacy named after A.P. Nelyubin, Sechenov University.
Project Description:
Project Goal: Development, optimization, and validation of modern methods for the qualitative and quantitative analysis of glycans in medicinal plant raw materials, herbal medicinal products, and herbal mixtures to improve the accuracy, selectivity, and reproducibility of pharmaceutical analysis.
Key Objectives:
‒	Development of a method for the quantitative determination of plant glycans by spectrophotometry using the anthrone reagent.
‒	Investigation of the polysaccharide complex (in particular, the qualitative and quantitative evaluation of inulin) by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) using appropriate detectors.
‒	Detailed profiling of simple carbohydrates (mono- and oligosaccharides) using capillary electrophoresis, which ensures high separation efficiency of complex matrix components.
‒	Metrological evaluation and validation of the developed methods (assessment of linearity, accuracy, and intralaboratory precision) for subsequent implementation into current regulatory documentation.

Scientific Project Title: "Use of light microscopy in the quality control of medicinal plant raw materials and medicinal plant preparations"  
Project team: Dr. Pharm. Sci., Associate Professor Bobkova N.V., postgraduate student: Pham Phuong Nam, students: Diana Bayramkulova, Darya Gadetskaya.
Project description: 
The goal of the project is to study the capabilities of various types of light microscopy (bright field, luminescent, polarizing, etc.) in the identification and quality control of medicinal plant raw materials and medicinal plant preparations (MPPs). 
Key tasks:
• Studying the influence of technological characteristics on the microscopic analysis of medicinal plant raw materials of various morphological groups. 
• Development of sample preparation methods for microscopic analysis of MPPs and solid dosage forms based on medicinal plant raw materials. 
• Study of anatomical and diagnostic features of related species of medicinal plants. 
• Use of luminescent and polarizing microscopy in the analysis of medicinal plant raw materials and medicinal products. Development of sample preparation methods. 
• Study of the possibility of using microchemical and histochemical reactions in the analysis of medicinal plant raw materials and MPPs.
